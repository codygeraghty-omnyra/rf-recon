What RF Recon is

RF Recon (beta) shows the radio picture of a place before you get there: every FCC-licensed frequency, tower and technology around a pin, predicted coverage by band, your own field captures, and the signals nothing explains.

It runs in-building public safety and cellular DAS jobs from the pre-survey to design, grid test, PIM and the AHJ report.

It comes as the PC app, a full web version, RF Recon lite for phones with no signal, and an always-on online version for invited people.

November 2, 2026

RF Recon goes online 24/7, the DAS tab gains a cellular side, a Screening step and example jobs, your keys leave the database, and RF Recon now says how far to trust each finding.48 changes

New

  • Online 24/7. RF Recon now runs around the clock for invited people, at its own web address. Each person has their own login with a role: View (look, search, leave notes), Full (also DAS jobs, missions and notes) or Admin (also keys, updates and people). Eight wrong passwords lock a name for 15 minutes.
  • Install it as an app. RF Recon installs as an app on a PC (the install icon in Chrome or Edge's address bar) or on a phone (Add to Home Screen).
  • DAS Screening step. A new step after Grid test judges each Grid test reading with its instrument's published accuracy: likely pass, too close to call or likely fail. It lists market instruments only: PCTEL IBflex, Anritsu S412E, Epiq PRiSM and Nextivity COMPASS XR. Readings marked not calibrated are listed but not judged. The Grid test score and the Report don't change.
  • DAS cellular side. Cellular coverage targets with presets (Common, Cooper, Providence normal and failover, SOLiD, MCF). The Grid test's Show picker colours one carrier, and its C-band or CBRS layer, by its best cell. Cellular results never count toward the public safety result.
  • COMPASS XR importer. Reads a Nextivity COMPASS XR Full Signal Report into the square you pick. Carrier names fill in from PLMN codes.
  • Carrier pre-survey. Cellular carriers at the building: FCC Broadband Map coverage, cell sites within 1 mile, a rooftop donor reading against the target, an approvals tracker and a consent letter per carrier (47 CFR 20.21(c)). The report gains a Commercial cellular coverage section.
  • Cellular link budget. Design adds LTE and 5G bands to the link budget, run in RSRP, with the consumer booster power caps.
  • DAS example jobs. Add example jobs makes two worked jobs: a public safety ERCES office at Acceptance and a carrier cellular warehouse at Design. On an example job, each step says what it's for, what to fill in and what to look at. Buildings, people and companies are fictional. The board also explains how a DAS job moves.
  • Update all. Data & updates gets Update all: every source checks now, selected or not, and each downloads only what's newer. Sources whose key isn't saved are skipped and named.
  • DAS marks. A DAS tag marks every source, key and setting in Data & updates that the DAS tab uses. DAS › Data & links gets an auto-update switch and Update all DAS data now.
  • Ways in. Data & updates lists every way to reach RF Recon or its data, and which ones work with Cody's PC off. Used live as you work lists the services the maps and searches call, with their terms.
  • Full-copy install kit. A second install kit carries the whole-US FCC data, carrier coverage, and the maps and terrain kept for no signal, so a new PC opens with everything and downloads nothing. It leaves out keys, logins, anyone's own work, crowd data, the Signal ID Wiki catalog and Artemis.
  • More looks. Display settings add nine colour skins (Ember, Moss, Tide, Midnight, Rosé, Graphite, Sand, Mint, Lilac), seven textures, five kinds of light and shadow, and six more accents.
  • How far to trust it. The Area report's signal list grades every finding in words (Confirmed, Probably true, Possibly true, Doubtful, Can't judge), with each source's own letter A to F and a one-line why, for example: ULS WQB268 (filing, as of 10/28) + capture (measured, as of 11/2). Confirmed: call sign decoded. Confirmed needs two independent sources agreeing on something that pins it down: a decoded call sign, or a level and a bearing that both fit the licensed site. Old rows fade.
  • Single source. A finding only one source backs carries a single source badge. Copies of one source count once: ten copies of one crowd report are one source, not ten confirmations.
  • Licensed, not heard. With captures that swept a band, the Area report lists FCC licences loud enough there that the sweep should have heard them, with nothing on their frequency: possibly silent, discontinued, blocked, or not keyed while you listened. A few spot readings don't count as a sweep.
  • New warning flags. A reading louder than its licence allows at that distance, a decoded call sign that isn't the licence's, and a bearing that points away from the licensed site are each flagged.
  • Receiver on each capture. Mission › Import field captures asks which receiver took them and whether it was calibrated; each capture's row shows it and can be edited. Calibrated levels grade A, uncalibrated C.
  • Satellite earth stations. A new map layer: the FCC's protected C-band earth stations (about 470), with where each dish points, its gain and height. On by default.
  • Pending microwave links. Microwave links filed with the FCC but not yet granted (about 2,300) draw dashed on the Microwave backhaul layer, with the filing number, purpose and date. Off by default: a 445 MB download.
  • Building outlines from Overture. When OpenStreetMap has no outline for a DAS building, or its servers don't answer, the outline comes from Overture Maps for areas with an Overture extract (Jacksonville NC, Wilmington NC and Shakopee MN so far). Overture also fills a building height OpenStreetMap lacks, marked as an estimate.
  • Sold build. The copy Josh sells (the install kit and the online server) leaves out every source whose terms bar selling or sharing: WiGLE, RepeaterBook, wspr.live, the Signal ID Wiki, Artemis and live ships. Cody's PC keeps them all. Help › Data credits and this build says which build you're on and what it leaves out.
  • Bearing error ellipse. Mission › lines of bearing draws where the transmitter probably is as a 95% error ellipse instead of a circle, and says its size, for example 368 m long by 260 m wide. It assumes bearings good to ±5°, or what the lines' own misses show when three or more disagree by more. Lines that cross at a shallow angle stretch it along them.
  • Carrier coverage vs your captures. Where a capture decoded cell IDs, the Area report compares what the FCC Broadband Map says each carrier covers with what was heard: Claimed, not heard (the capture decoded other carriers there, but none of that one's) and Weaker than claimed (more than 10 dB under the map). The map is modelled outdoors, so indoors these are often expected.
  • Track record. Pooled across every mission, the Area report counts how often each source's claims held up against your own captures: FCC licences heard vs licensed but not heard, and crowd cells or radios your captures also decoded vs ones near where you listened that they didn't. After 20 outcomes a source earns its grade (A to E), marked ✓, in place of the proposed one.

Improved

  • Every channel counts. A Grid test square keeps every reading, one per channel or system. The worst calibrated reading decides, so a square passes only when every channel does. Imports keep channel, position and cellular fields and merge by channel. All readings CSV exports and imports back.
  • Floor plans. The plan keeps its own shape. Place grid puts, sizes and turns the grid on it, and the square size comes from the plan's width.
  • Too close to call. The example job's square 3-5 now reads 0.5 dB over the line, so Screening shows it as too close to call.
  • Consumer booster bands. The carrier pre-survey warns about bands a consumer booster may not carry: 600 MHz, WCS, 2.5 GHz, CBRS, 3.45 GHz / C-band and AWS-4, with notes per band. It also says how each carrier takes consent, and the consent letter asks what T-Mobile's form asks.
  • Cooper preset. Matches the spec's Table 2 in full: T-Mobile C-band at -100 dBm, LTE 1900 and 2100 per carrier, no SINR target. Each band must pass on its own.
  • Maps with no signal. Only the USGS maps and county lines are kept for use with no signal. Street and Esri satellite maps need signal, per their terms. With no signal the map switches to USGS Topo and goes back to your pick when signal returns. On the PC, online, web and lite.
  • Keys sealed. API keys are no longer stored in any database file. On the PC they're sealed to your Windows account. On first start, older keys move over and the old copy is wiped from the file.
  • Saves during a data refresh. Saves no longer fail for minutes while the FCC data refreshes. If one still can't go through, RF Recon says so plainly. On the online server, heavy data refreshes run at night, 2 to 5 AM Eastern.
  • Crowd history kept. When a crowd source changes a row on a later download, the old version is kept instead of overwritten.
  • Download progress. Data & updates shows each step of an FCC licence rebuild instead of only rebuilding database.

Fixed

  • Second request failed. A second request in a row, such as a second DAS delete, could fail with error 501. Proven fixed on live.
  • Fast DAS switching. Switching DAS screens fast drew onto the wrong screen and threw an error. Proven fixed on live.
  • Web Design tab. The web version's Design tab now shows the floor plan, and plans show in its Grid test too. Proven fixed on live.
  • DAQ 3.4 pass line. Now SINR 18 dB / BER 2%. It was 19 dB, a figure from another radio type's row in TSB-88. Requirements shows the job's own DAQ equivalent.
  • Cellular readings. LTE and 5G readings (RSRP, RSRQ) were scored as public safety downlink levels. Now they're kept and shown but never scored against the public safety rules.
  • Web source tables. The web version's data source tables no longer sit on Loading.
  • DAS screens left while loading. DAS Data & links and Plans no longer throw an error when left or redrawn while loading.
  • Carrier coverage on the server. A carrier coverage download stopped with database is locked when someone looked at the coverage map while a file loaded. Loading and reading no longer block each other.
  • Failed downloads retry. A source whose download fails tries again after 2 hours. It used to wait out its whole interval: 30 days for carrier coverage.

Data

  • DAS cellular research. Cellular presets, consent steps and the booster band rules come from new research, checked against the current FCC rule (47 CFR 20.21(e)(3), unchanged since 2018) and the FCC's own grant list.
  • Esri imagery credit. Esri's satellite imagery is now credited to Vantor.
  • Data notices. Reports carry the FCC's notice (this product uses the FCC Data API but is not endorsed or certified by the FCC), the OpenCelliD credit where its towers appear, and the OpenStreetMap or Overture credit with a building outline.
  • Signal ID Wiki terms. The wiki grants no licence to reuse its content, so the web version no longer carries its copy. Cody or Josh settles it with the wiki's owners.

Where it is

  • PC app. Runs today's version, as the personal build.
  • Online server. Runs the morning's version; it gets the trust grades, the new layers and the sold build after its first full-US download finishes. It is a separate copy: a DAS job made on one isn't on the other.
  • Web version. Has the DAS changes, the example jobs, the fixes and the trust grades, as the sold build.
  • Install kits. Installed copies offer the update in Data & updates.

  • Web Packs error. A Packs screen drawn after you'd already left it no longer throws an error.

  • Grant imports. Lines from several searches now add up instead of replacing each other, a device keeps every class it holds, a one-record file reads, and the FCC's files with a bare & read.

Data

  • DAS and booster makers' grants. Each grant line's frequency range for 16 makers' 20 codes, including ADRF, Fiplex, Comba, SOLiD, CommScope, Corning, Nextivity, SureCall and weBoost: 1,244 devices and 5,178 lines.

Where it is

October 29, 2026

DAS design and link budgets, mission replay and bearings, state packs on the RF Recon site, and DAS on the phone.18 changes

New

  • DAS Design. A link budget per band (downlink, uplink, code and 47 CFR 90.219 checks), antennas and walls on the plans with predicted coverage from measured wall losses, and battery sizing. The Report adds a design submittal.
  • Mission replay. Replay a mission's readings on a map with play, speed and how far back to show. Quiet stretches are skipped.
  • When heard. An hour-by-weekday wheel shows when signals are heard.
  • Lines of bearing. Bearings from the field log or a capture's bearing column draw lines and show where they cross, with the miss distance. The phone's field log takes a bearing too.
  • Artemis waterfalls and audio. Each Signal ID Wiki match shows its waterfall and plays its audio. The Artemis download is off by default and stays on the PC.
  • State packs. One pack per state on the RF Recon site. The web version loads the one around a search by itself, and the phone app offers it. Both list every state.
  • DAS on the phone. Grid tests square by square with no signal, using the PC's AHJ profiles and pass rules, exported as a CSV the PC's Grid test reads. Also a PIM calculator and the code numbers.
  • Phone display settings. The phone app gets the same display settings as the PC: Match system, skins, accent, backdrop photo, glass, reduce transparency and motion.

Improved

  • Link budget defaults. Each band starts from cited defaults (portable power, antenna and body loss, site sensitivity, fade margin and more). New checks: the uplink level window at the site, uplink over sensitivity, server ERP, donor port power, uplink gain cap, booster noise at the site and an isolation warning. A cleared box goes back to its default.
  • Over-driven booster. The link budget says how far over its per-channel maximum an over-driven booster is.
  • DAS Data & links. Now covers design references, AHJ guidelines with their limits, PIM and parts, FCC ID and Broadband Map look-ups, and four new research sets.
  • Critical areas on import. A critical-area column in a grid import marks those squares critical.
  • Kit update notes. Read cleanly: cut at a comma, never mid-word.
  • Area report. The coverage note now says it uses terrain, as the map has since October 28.

Fixed

  • Kit updates not seen. Installed copies couldn't see a newer kit. Now they can.
  • Reload after an update. The page waits for the copy to report the new version before it reloads, and the version card retries once after a restart.
  • State packs. A site with only a latitude no longer fails a state's pack, and one failed pack no longer blocks the next.

Data

  • Field research. Cited research sets for PIM, RF parts and suppliers, building plans and wall materials, and link budgets, opened from DAS Data & links.

October 28, 2026

The web version, ATAK, the DAS jobs tab, the install kit and a long list of new layers and sources.45 changes

New

  • Full web version. Every PC tab runs in a browser from a field pack, with map tiles fetched from their sources. It keeps what it fetched for use with no signal.
  • ATAK export. A TAK data package of what's on screen: a marker per site coloured by signal type, the search pin and ring, and the coverage heatmap. From the PC app, the web version and the phone app (Send to ATAK / iTAK).
  • Push to ATAK. Sends live markers to every TAK app on the PC's networks, and to a TAK Server if one is set. On a phone or the web version, the share sheet hands ATAK the package.
  • Map styles. Standard, ATAK style (2525-style frames, imagery, MGRS readout) or your own map (an ATAK or WinTAK map source, a tile address, MBTiles). RF Recon's maps export as ATAK map sources.
  • DAS jobs tab. In-building public safety radio (ERCES / BDA / DAS) from lead to annual retest: a board of jobs by stage, contacts, compliance dates and an AHJ profile per job, with links to the DAS research.
  • DAS pre-survey. From the FCC data: the public safety systems around the building, downlink and uplink channels, ESMR, Band 14 and simulcast flags, the strongest sites, levels outside and inside, the bands a booster must pass, a channel plan and consent letters (47 CFR 90.219).
  • DAS grid test and AHJ report. A grid per floor over the floor plan, readings typed or imported from any tool, pass or fail by the AHJ profile, cell-size warnings and an acceptance baseline for the annual retest. The AHJ report prints the summary, each floor's plan and readings, the tester and instruments. Files go to a folder per job in Drive.
  • PIM and Parts tabs. Plus DAS Plans, Materials and PIM sections. PDF plan sheets load as floor plans, with a wall loss model.
  • Emitter details. Click any emitter for its coverage (with the antenna pattern and hills) and what it's networked with: base stations and mobiles, channel pairs, simulcast, the tower, microwave links and talk groups.
  • Cell sites tab. Crowd and survey cells decoded to global cell IDs, grouped into sites and matched to FCC towers, with estimated sector directions.
  • Carrier coverage. Each carrier's modelled 3G, 4G and 5G coverage with signal level, from the FCC Broadband Map, for chosen states: a heatmap by carrier and technology, and the carriers at the pin in Cell sites.
  • Search everything. One box over everything (the Search tab, or Ctrl+K), grouped and nearest first. It reads 'Charlotte fire' as town plus kind and shows one row per licensee. DAS Find searches every field in every job.
  • Pin lock. Clicks select dots instead of moving the pin. A clicked dot lights up in the list and table and opens On this spot under the map.
  • Measured heatmap. By survey file and by signal.
  • New map layers. Live ships (AIS), 911 call centres (the FCC's PSAP registry) and microwave backhaul links (the FCC's path records).
  • FCC ID look-up. The FCC's equipment authorization records by grantee code or FCC ID, with the maker behind any FCC ID, also offline.
  • Install kit. RF Recon for another PC, with its own Python. Its welcome screen picks states and downloads the FCC data. It finds a free port and installs newer kits from inside the app.
  • Remote access. A signed-in, view-only way into the PC version, with people managed in Data & updates. Built and kept switched off.
  • Display settings. A settings sheet with skin cards, accent, backdrop photo, glass, reduce transparency, motion and data downloads.
  • Back and Close buttons. Beside the Look button, since RF Recon's window has no browser back button.
  • Your API keys. A row per service: what it unlocks, saved or not, last fetch, where to get a key, and remove.
  • Packs of several areas. One field pack can hold several states and circles.

Improved

  • Data & updates. Update over All, Wi-Fi, Ethernet or cellular in one place, why each source isn't downloading, and the key, cost and terms of each source.
  • Heatmaps. Predicted heatmaps use terrain, and AM and HF are left out of predictions. With no pin, a county heatmap covers the county. An empty heatmap says why.
  • Merged emitters. The same emitter from several sources shows once, with the most trusted source leading.
  • Map filters fold away. Collapse hides them, and the header says how many are on.
  • Map zoom. - and + buttons and a mouse-wheel zoom switch, side by side.
  • Look. Depth and beveled edges on panels and buttons, smoother motion, and Match system (light by day, dark at night) as the default skin.
  • Opens from anywhere. Opening the app's page from the folder goes to the running app. The server can start with Windows. The phone app on a computer points to the full app.
  • Table downloads. Display settings name each table's CSV by its heading.
  • Carrier coverage downloads. A download must be whole or it's tried again. The next run fetches only the files that failed.

Fixed

  • Map clicks. Clicks now reach the dots on the map, also through a locked pin.
  • Settings stuck open. The Look panel and display settings close when you pick an option or press outside them.
  • Capture import. The summary survives the search that follows, and leaving the tab mid-import is safe.
  • Terminal pop-ups. No more Windows Terminal windows popping up from the network check.
  • Tab bar. No longer covers the toolbar under it.
  • Licensee categories. Match whole words: Firebird isn't fire, Marshall isn't police.
  • State-limited imports. Keep microwave receive ends filed with no state. 911 centres wait for the town list. The welcome screen retries only what failed.

Data

  • FCC Broadband Map. Carrier coverage per state, with a free FCC key.
  • FCC equipment grantee codes. FCC open data, no key: the maker behind any FCC ID.
  • Artemis. The Artemis signal database: Signal ID Wiki signals with waterfalls and audio.
  • Building outlines and terrain. OpenStreetMap building outlines and public terrain tiles, for DAS and coverage.

Where it is

October 27, 2026

RF Recon (beta) arrives: the PC app, map layers, the Area report, the spectrum view, broadcast, NOAA and FAA layers, and the first phone app.41 changes

New

  • RF Recon (beta). The first version: FCC licences and towers around a pin, with Map, Analyzer, Dashboard, Topology, Site profile, Agencies, Spectrum, Mission and Data & updates tabs, in its own window on the PC.
  • Area report. Everything licensed around a pin: a one-sentence summary that follows the filters, tiles you can drill into, a bands table, predicted coverage per band, technologies and every licensed frequency. It prints.
  • Unlisted signals. Every capture and crowd point is checked against FCC licences on that frequency within 40 miles and against known unlicensed uses. What's left is Unlisted, sorted first and drawn in red.
  • Map layers. Street, USGS satellite, topo and terrain relief, Esri hi-res satellite, county and state lines, and live NEXRAD radar. Your choice is remembered.
  • Address search. The pin box takes addresses, places and counties, with suggestions as you type. Towns and counties work offline.
  • Nationwide search. Licence search anywhere by call sign, licensee, service or frequency, with FCC search fields: presets, radio service codes, technology, emission, and grant and expiry dates.
  • Links to a view. The tab, pin, radius, county and filters live in the address, so a reload or a shared link restores the view.
  • Spectrum view. Exact frequencies down to kHz with channel widths, zoom and pan, a band-plan strip, a log axis for wide ranges, and markers with deltas.
  • Signal ID Wiki. What a signal could be, in every detail window, the Area report's unlisted findings, the field log and the Spectrum tab. Kept on the PC for offline look-ups.
  • Help. The ? button or F1: 16 sections and a search box. It opens on the current tab's section.
  • Broadcast and NOAA layers. AM, FM and TV stations, and NOAA Weather Radio transmitters. A county search returns the transmitters that warn that county.
  • FAA aviation layer. Every assigned aviation frequency with its facility and use, plus navaids and ILS.
  • Compare two surveys. Pick a baseline mission and a dB threshold to see what's new, gone and changed.
  • RadioReference links. Every licence links to its RadioReference call-sign and state pages.
  • Glass look. Smoke, Haze, Dusk, Liquid and Daylight skins, four glass densities and one Look button for skin, glass and motion.
  • Phone app and field packs. An offline phone app with map, list, area report and a field log over one area's data. Field packs are made from Data & updates. The field log exports a CSV the PC imports.
  • Backups. Your own work (never API keys) is backed up every 6 hours.

Improved

  • Markings. Dashboard charts get axes, medians and dated ticks. The map gets a scale bar, labelled distance rings and a cursor readout with distance and bearing from the pin. Agencies sort and filter, with Band and Nearest columns.
  • Analyzer. Sort order, rows per page, a column chooser, a row-colour legend, and Alt+click to filter a cell in or out.
  • Topology. The plain mouse wheel scrolls the page; Ctrl+wheel or pinch zooms. Zoom buttons, find a node, layouts, full screen and a legend.
  • Data freshness. Every source shows how old its data is and when the next check comes.
  • Smaller updates. FCC licence and tower updates download only files newer than the saved copy.
  • Phone tether. A phone tethered over USB counts as cellular, so Wi-Fi-only updates wait.
  • Filtered towers. With a frequency or text filter on, towers show only when they carry a matching licence.
  • Connection status. Recovers by itself after a brief dropout.
  • Tap targets. Controls are at least 44 pixels on phones.
  • Bands. 54-72 MHz is VHF TV low and 76-108 MHz is FM broadcast.

Fixed

  • Startup crash. RF Recon no longer crashes on start on some Windows setups.
  • Re-imports. Every FCC import after the first failed, including automatic updates. Now they work.
  • Tower owners. Owner names come from the right field, without the FCC ID glued on.
  • County search for towers. Towers now carry their county, so county searches find them.
  • FCC downloads. When the FCC refuses the app's downloader, it uses Windows' own curl. Automatic updates no longer get stuck after the first import.
  • Search. A street address like 7400 22nd St no longer reads as coordinates, and a slow search can't overwrite a newer one.
  • Stale app files. After an update the browser no longer runs old code.
  • Missions. Saving a mission deleted in another window says so instead of failing.
  • Phone app. Search suggestions close when the pin moves.

Data

  • Census county codes. Give every tower its county.
  • FCC Media Bureau and National Weather Service. 59,150 AM, FM and TV transmitters, and 1,035 NOAA Weather Radio transmitters with the counties each warns.
  • FAA aviation data. The FAA's 28-day aviation frequency data, checked weekly.
  • Signal ID Wiki catalog. 599 signals, refreshed monthly.
  • Street addresses. Photon (OpenStreetMap), US only.
