What RF Recon is

RF Recon (beta) shows the radio picture of a place before you get there: every FCC-licensed frequency, tower and technology around a pin, predicted coverage by band, your own field captures, and the signals nothing explains.

It runs in-building public safety and cellular DAS jobs from the pre-survey to design, grid test, PIM and the AHJ report.

It lives on an always-on server for invited people, with a PC app for work with no signal, a full web version and RF Recon lite for phones.

November 4, 2026, late

Built to beat the DAS market tools: AHJ rules read from 16 AHJs' own policies, an AHJ packet checklist, acceptance tests, signed and sealed reports; guided commissioning and sweeps; design tuned to the walk test, floor to floor, per antenna, battery by the AHJ's rule and a 15-sheet submittal; no lost edits, history and access per job; filing deadlines and reminders; FCC boosters as parts; the web version offline; an Android app that reads the phone and corrects it against a calibrated instrument; Find a signal coaching; RF need for every prospect; finished example jobs.43 changes

New

  • AHJ rules with their sources. Requirements: 16 presets read from each AHJ's own policy (San Diego, LA County, Clark County, Seattle, Colorado Springs, Miami-Dade, Osceola, Santa Clara, Murray, Charlotte, Denver, Riverside, Durham, College Station, Salt Lake City, Douglas County). SINR, BER and uplink-in-every-square come from the policy, and Where each number comes from shows the section, or says the policy doesn't state it.
  • AHJ packet checklist. Report: what the AHJ asks for (20 lists, 244 items, each with its section), what the job already has, and what's missing before you print. The packet index prints at the top of the report.
  • Acceptance tests. Grid test: the 12 tests IFC 510.5.4 and NFPA 1225 ask for (gain, filters and AGC, isolation, noise floor, oscillation, two-radio, 1-hour battery, supervisory signals, EPO, leakage, donor level, BDA identity), each pass / fail / not done, printed on the report.
  • Sign and seal the report. Report: the tester's name, statement, independence statement and drawn signature. Print as final seals it: a verify code and page number on every page, and DAS › Verify a report shows whether the job changed since.
  • Calibration certificates. Overview › Testing: each instrument's certificate and expiry. Warned 30 days ahead; readings after expiry stop counting; certificates go into the AHJ packet.
  • Guided commissioning. A Commissioning step: the NFPA 1225 A.18.9.3 donor-site noise test, isolation (20 dB over gain), gain / AGC / filter settings, oscillation, donor level and a talk-back log, with the AHJ's own numbers where it has them. Results fill the acceptance tests.
  • Sweeps. PIM / sweeps: import return loss, VSWR, distance to fault and insertion loss from Anritsu, FieldFox, Touchstone or a plain table; plotted and graded, each limit with its source.
  • Test passes. Walk: BDA off, BDA on, acceptance, annual and five-year passes. Compare any two as a difference heat map, with Clark County's 5% check.
  • One grid, two technologies. Report: per floor, the public-safety map and the cellular map side by side, a table per square, separate verdicts.
  • Cellular jobs judged as cellular. A carrier-only job's report is a commercial cellular coverage test, judged per carrier; its Grid test opens on its carrier; its lifecycle is the carriers' consents and terms, not IFC 510.
  • Design tuned to the walk. Antennas and coverage: every walk point against the prediction, then Fit to the walk fits the path loss, each wall type and the floor loss to your readings.
  • Floor to floor. Antennas on the floors above and below count through ITU-R P.1238 floor loss.
  • Per-antenna budget and caps. Each antenna's port power, ERP against the cap, weakest point and uplink to the agency; caps presets for Douglas County, Sacramento FPS 32, LA County and San Diego.
  • Battery by the AHJ's rule. Battery only or with a generator, hours from the rule, the 70% low-battery point, and the Colorado Springs or FDNY table format.
  • 15-sheet design submittal. Cover sheet, statement of compliance, sheet index, donor path, isolation, test grids on the plans, materials, the walk check, per-antenna budget and qualifications; revision and page on every page.
  • DXF plans. Import a DXF floor plan: it becomes the plan, and wall-layer lines are offered as walls with a material from each layer.
  • Building loss by ITU-R P.2109. Pre-survey: the loss for this building from its year and size, in place of a fixed 15 dB.
  • No lost edits. Two people changing one job: different fields merge; the same field asks Keep theirs / Keep yours / See both.
  • History. Log › History: every change with who and when, Undo, and Restore to here.
  • Who can open a job. Log › Sharing: the whole team, or named logins at view, edit or AHJ review. AHJ reviewer logins and read-only links are built and switched off.
  • Filing deadlines. Lifecycle: when the test report is due to the AHJ (OCSD 30 days and 13 months, Seattle 7, Charleston 30, Denver) and local five-year rules, each with its source.
  • Reminders. Ready to send: every lifecycle date as a written email 90 and 30 days ahead, on the day and while overdue; nothing sends without a click.
  • BDA monitoring. Overview › Monitoring: SNMP alarms from any maker's BDA, in NFPA 1225's words. Built and switched off: it needs a firewall port opened on the server.
  • Boosters from FCC grants. Materials: every Part 90 Class A and B booster on the FCC's grants as a part, and AHJ code fields (UL 2524, class, FCC ID, NEMA, 2-hour, PIM) on every part, in the BOM and the packet.
  • Antenna patterns. Planet .msi / .pln files become antenna parts with their pattern.
  • The web version with no signal. The full web version keeps everything it needs, its engine included (16.5 MB), so a DAS job can be made and saved in a basement.
  • RF Recon for Android. Help › Android app: download and install steps. The walk test reads the phone's own LTE / 5G signal.
  • Phone correction. Walk › Phone vs instrument: at a few spots, the phone and a calibrated instrument side by side give a table per band; the phone's readings are corrected from then on. Still not calibrated: never counted toward pass / fail.
  • Find a signal coaching. A bearing saves only when the compass is steady; from the 4th stop it says where to stand next; a stop counter; from 8 bearings the bad ones are left out. Simulated median miss 58 m to 18 m.
  • RF need for every prospect. Prospects: whether each building likely falls short inside (the deciding agency system, the building's loss, each carrier), sorted need first.
  • Address boxes finish the address. A job's address, the firm's mailing address and Prospects' Where offer the full address with ZIP as you type.
  • Finished example jobs. Add example jobs adds a finished ERCES office and a finished cellular warehouse, In service, beside the two mid-way ones.
  • Ways in: versions. Data & updates › Ways in shows each version's build, and RF Recon for Android.

Fixed

  • Uplink in every square. Where the AHJ requires uplink in every square, a square with only a downlink reading is untested, not a pass.
  • DAQ 3.4 SINR. The SINR / BER bar comes from the AHJ (San Diego and LA County 20 dB / 2%), not a fixed 18 dB.
  • Battery hours on the submittal. It printed 12 h whatever the job used.
  • Personal-only sources. WiGLE, RepeaterBook, Signal ID Wiki and live ships no longer reach team logins or the sold build.
  • Area report. Print gave a blank page; the Signals section failed on some readings.
  • Analyzer and Search. Keep only this works on towers, 911 centres and Unknown; a frequency under Broadcast, aviation, NOAA finds them; an analyzer CSV with no coordinates is placed from the GPS track.
  • Needs you. Past 8 items, Show all opens the rest (they were hidden).
  • Quote requests. A new request could inherit an old one's emails; ids are never reused now.
  • Prospects after a restart. The first list and RF need are warmed at start.
  • Help. Matches the code, with new sections: Search, Cell sites, Find a signal, Replay, Compare, Receivers, Android app.

A walk test on the floor plan with heat maps; emails RF Recon writes and lines up for a click; pick who from your contacts with a preview every time; FCC licence changes watched on every job; passband licensees, intermod, existing-system audits, an AHJ guess and tunnels; All / None on every checklist.15 changes

New

  • Walk test. Grid test › Walk: tap where you stand on the plan, with a mouse or on a phone. Type the values at each spot, load a COMPASS Full Signal Report, or walk continuously and import the instrument's or a phone survey app's log (G-NetTrack and others; any CSV through a column picker): each reading is placed by its time between your taps. A heat map per metric and per carrier against the AHJ's threshold or the job's cellular target; the walk rolls into the grid squares, so the IFC score and the Report use it (only calibrated instruments count). Points CSV, heat map PNG, and heat maps in the Report.
  • Ready to send. DAS › Mail lists emails RF Recon wrote on its own: follow-ups when a tagged email got no reply in 5 days, retest reminders to the owner from 30 days before, plans requests for jobs with no plans, and first emails queued from Prospects (✉ Queue first emails, up to 50). Preview one to read or edit it, or tick several and Send selected; Later puts one off a week. Nothing goes without your click, and a mailbox sends at most 150 an hour.
  • Pick who, then preview. Email boxes offer the job's people by role, every contact and supplier, and anyone who replied; the email is written for that kind of message, greets them by first name and shows as a preview with Edit and Send. Email about this job has a Kind of email menu: a status update to the customer, access and schedule to the general contractor, scope and price to a subcontractor (a new role), the design to the AHJ, the plans request and the retest reminder. Save as contact for new addresses and replies; Import contacts reads Google and Outlook exports and vCards.
  • FCC licence changes. Each job's licences are snapshotted and checked after every FCC refresh: a licence gone or expired, renamed, channels or sites changed, expiring within 90 days, or a new licensee on or next to the job's channels shows in Needs you, a banner on the job and the Pre-survey, with why it matters at that stage.
  • Licensees inside the booster's passbands. Every licensee whose channels near the building fall inside a passband (downlink and uplink apart), whether a Class B booster or a 75 kHz Class A filter passes it, its FCC contact and its consent status, with a letter or an email each (47 CFR 90.219). In Arlington, VA, the County's VHF and 800 MHz systems pass 29 licensees.
  • Consent letters to the right address. Letters and consent emails go to the licence's own contact from the FCC's records, and say so; with no record, nothing is guessed.
  • Intermod. 3rd and 5th order products of the plan's channels, checked against the uplinks and Class A filters: frequencies to watch and a PIM test plan per band in PIM, a summary in the link budget.
  • Existing system. A new job tab for a booster already in the building: what's on file, a 14-item sourced checklist, and findings (unregistered Class B, consent missing, channels outside its passbands, Band 14 or ESMR passed, old UL 2524, no oscillation shutdown, overdue test) that reach Needs you and the AHJ report.
  • AHJ guess. The fire department covering the job's address from the USFA National Fire Department Registry (it stopped taking updates in June 2026, and says so): the city's inside a city, the county's outside; Use as AHJ fills the job.
  • Tunnels. Road and rail tunnels from OpenStreetMap as a Prospects type and near each building in the Pre-survey (NFPA 502 / 130).
  • All / None on every checklist. Material rules, PIM checklists, critical areas, pre-survey systems, carriers, suppliers and building types: one box ticks or clears the list (a dash when some are ticked), the header shows how many, and long lists fold like the map's filters until opened. The report says how many material rules are confirmed.

Fixed

  • Battery hours. Battery sizing starts from the job's AHJ hours (24 for the strict presets) instead of always 12.
  • Deleting a job file. The warning says what really happens: on the server and the web version a deleted file is gone; on the PC, Drive keeps it 30 days.
  • Phones. A long folder path no longer pushes the DAS and Data screens sideways on a phone.
  • Mail sign-in. A pasted address with invisible characters is cleaned; Google's own reason is shown when it refuses; an app password copied with spaces works; an empty password is caught first.

November 4, 2026, evening

Walls drawn from the plan's own lines, with snip, lasso and smart materials; email sent and answered inside RF Recon from your own mailbox; a wheel-zoom lock on every map; grid scoring to the IFC rule; realistic pre-survey levels; lifecycle dates; nursing homes, a donor aiming sheet and the national building inventory.18 changes

New

  • Walls from the plan itself. Design › Antennas and coverage reads the floor plan's own lines: from the PDF when the plan came from one (every wall found on the test drawings and the example jobs), from the image otherwise (97% on a clean plan image, 87% on a blurred scan; a phone photo finds none and says draw by hand). Drag along a wall or click its ends; the pointer snaps to wall ends, the plan's line ends and crossings, onto its lines and square to the plan, and a double-line wall is drawn on its centre.
  • Snip, lasso and Smart. Snip (S) is a box, Lasso (L) any loop, a circle too: the walls inside are found at once in the wall type picked. Smart (Shift+S, Shift+L) gives each wall its own type from what RF Recon knows: a word on the plan beside it (CMU, CONC, GYP, CURTAIN WALL), stair, elevator and shaft boxes, glazing drawn as three lines, hatched or solid fills and the wall's thickness, outside walls from the job's other floors, its construction and the building type. What's found is shown dashed, green sure, amber check, grey probably not, each with why; Enter adds the sure ones. From a PDF where every find is sure, they go straight in, one Ctrl+Z away.
  • The best tool for each plan. A 💡 button names the tool that suits this plan (Smart snip for a clean PDF, Snip then review, Draw with snap, or Draw by hand when no lines can be read); click it to switch.
  • Keyboard. W walls, A antennas, S snip, L lasso, E remove; hold Shift in any tool to remove, in Remove to draw; M and Shift+M step the wall type, 1-9 and 0 pick one, B and F step band and floor; X snap; Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z undo and redo, 30 steps per floor; [ and ] the previous and next floor on Design, Grid test and Screening. A key strip under the toolbar and Help › Walls on floor plans list them.
  • Email inside RF Recon. Connect your own mailbox once (Gmail, Yahoo, iCloud, AOL, Zoho, Fastmail, GMX or any IMAP/SMTP host, with an app password; each signed-in person has their own). Quote requests, prospect emails, plans requests, licensee and carrier consent requests, the AHJ report and retest reminders go from RF Recon with a short tag in the subject, [RFR …], and land in your Sent folder. Replies carrying the tag come back into the job, the quote or the prospect: a supplier's quote sheet is read in and typed prices are offered to apply, a plans reply's files land in the job, a 'no thanks' marks the prospect Do not contact, and a reply from an address you never wrote to is held. Nothing is sent until you press Send; untagged mail is never read. Microsoft accounts need sign-in RF Recon doesn't have yet, and the web and phone versions keep Open in my mail.
  • A wheel-zoom lock on every map. Beside − and + on every map, a lock: when it's on, the mouse wheel scrolls the page and only − and + zoom. One setting for every map, remembered; the phone app's maps have it too.
  • Grid scoring to the IFC rule. A floor of 20 areas fails with more than one failed area; with exactly two, Re-divide into 40 areas keeps the readings and starts the 40, which pass with at most two failed areas that don't touch. Percentages are cut, not rounded, so a fail never shows 95.0%. The floor, the grid and the report say which rule failed the floor and why.
  • Realistic pre-survey levels. Beside the free-space levels, an estimate from Okumura-Hata (suburban by default) with the licensed antenna height and pattern and the terrain along cached tiles. The verdict uses it and says so: in Arlington, VA, 24 of 43 systems come out below −95 dBm inside, where free space found none.
  • Lifecycle dates. Each job tracks the annual test (rolled forward from the last one entered), the NFPA 1225 five-year retest, licensee renewals, the operating permit, the UL 2524 3rd-edition check and a retest after a remodel, each with its source, in the Overview and in Needs you.
  • Donor aiming sheet. For each donor site: true and magnetic bearing, elevation angle allowing for the earth's curve, distance, terrain clearance, simulcast and whether the building is off the site antenna's main beam; printable, and in the ATAK export.
  • The building from the national inventory. Look up the job's building in the USACE National Structure Inventory: occupancy, stories, square feet, basement, year built, height and people by day and night, as estimates with their source. It fills only empty fields.
  • Nursing homes in Prospects. From the CMS provider file, with beds, ownership, chain and sprinkler status, beside schools and hospitals.
  • Six more AHJ presets. Sacramento Metro, McKinney, Prosper, Chandler, NYC FDNY ARCS and Florida 9th edition, each with its source; and the consent processes of nine licensees.

Fixed

  • A new job could show a deleted job's files. Job numbers are never reused, and a deleted job's folder is kept, renamed '_deleted <date> …'.
  • Band 14 wording. Band 14 is licensed to the FirstNet Authority, which gives the written retransmission authorization; AT&T runs the network. Said so everywhere it appears.
  • Research corrections. Market, link budget and materials figures corrected from the November research, each with its source; vendor figures and search excerpts are marked as such.
  • Saving a plan page twice. A page saved again now points the floor at the new image, not the old one.
  • The server log. A browser leaving mid-answer is no longer logged as an error, and Build information names the deployed commit.

Where it is

  • Server. Deployed and checked on the server (24b942f): the view-only checks pass (36 of 36), and on the example office job's floor 1 the plan's lines are read, a snip finds the walls in its box and Esc leaves the job as it was. Mail on the server is per signed-in person; no mailbox is connected yet.
  • Web and phone. Published (build 227c8b77b4): the zoom lock and the wall tools are in the web version and the zoom lock in the phone app. DAS Mail there says it works on the server and the PC app, and each email button opens your own mail app.
  • Tested. 222 unit tests pass, including wall-finding accuracy on drawn test plans and the example jobs' plans; on a test copy with a stand-in mailbox, the full sweep passes 755 of 766 checks with 11 known warnings (test-copy folder names shown, small text links on phones), and the mail, walls, zoom-lock and research groups pass in full.

Costs
