# RF Recon: RF/DAS materials, components and suppliers

Research for the Parts tab and the DAS job Materials section. Compiled 2026-10-29. Every number below comes from a datasheet, maker page or code text that was opened; each row links its source. Where a sheet did not give a value it is left blank (`-`), not interpolated, except where noted for Times Microwave cables (computed from the formula printed on the Times datasheet).

Data file: `components.json` in the same folder (keys: sources, cables, connectors, components, suppliers, das, checklists). Counts: 209 sources, 26 cables, 9 connectors, 21 component types, 35 suppliers, 22 code/design rows, 5 checklists.

## What changed in the industry (read first)

- **CommScope is no longer the HELIAX/DAS company.** Amphenol completed its purchase of CommScope's Outdoor Wireless Networks and DAS businesses on Feb 3, 2025 ([sup_amphenol_own_das_close](https://investors.amphenol.com/news-and-events/news-details/2025/Amphenol-Corporation-Completes-Acquisition-of-OWN-and-DAS-Businesses-From-CommScope/default.aspx)); those lines (HELIAX, ERA, ION) now sell as ANDREW, an Amphenol company. Datasheets in this report still carry the CommScope name because they predate the sale.
- **RFS** North America is "RFS Technologies, an Amphenol company" ([sup_rfs_amphenol_pr](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/articles/press-releases/rfs-technologies-an-amphenol-company)); its datasheets now live on rfstechnologies.com.
- **Times Microwave** and **PCTEL** are Amphenol companies ([sup_aph_q1_2009_tms](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000820313/000110465909024515/a09-10274_1ex99d1.htm), [sup_pctel_amphenol_close](https://pctel.com/news/pctel-announces-closing-of-its-acquisition-by-amphenol-corporation/)). **Microlab** belongs to RF Industries ([sup_mwj_rfil_microlab_close](https://www.microwavejournal.com/articles/37781-rf-industries-completes-acquisition-of-microlab)). **Corning** sold its DAS business to Airspan in 2025 ([sup_fastmode_airspan_close](https://www.thefastmode.com/solution-vendors-m-a/40705-airspan-finalizes-acquisition-of-corning-s-wireless-business-expanding-5g-portfolio)). **Zinwave** is part of Wilson Connectivity ([sup_wilson_zinwave_2023](https://markets.financialcontent.com/stocks/article/bizwire-2023-1-5-wilson-electronics-acquires-uk-based-zinwave-now-delivers-globally-the-most-comprehensive-fiber-based-wireless-coverage-solution)). **SureCall's** public-safety line went to Potter ([sup_surecall_potter](https://surecall.com/public-safety/)).

### Notes by cable family

- **LDF4-50A** (HELIAX LDF (1/2 in low-density foam, corrugated copper), CommScope HELIAX (now ANDREW, an Amphenol company)). General 1/2 in feeder and jumpers outdoors (roof donor runs); indoors only where the code allows an unlisted PE jacket (e.g. in metal raceway). 1-8800 MHz; 40 kW peak; 0.815 dB/100 ft at 150 MHz. 850/1900/2600 MHz are not tabulated; nearest listed rows given instead (no interpolation). Sheet dated 2018.
- **LDF4RK-50A** (HELIAX LDF 1/2 in, fire-retardant jacket, CommScope HELIAX (now ANDREW)). In-building riser (vertical, floor-to-floor) and general runs where a CATVR/CMR cable is accepted; not for plenum air-handling spaces. Same attenuation table as LDF4-50A on the sheet. 1-8800 MHz.
- **HL4RPV-50** (HELIAX plenum air dielectric 1/2 in, CommScope HELIAX (now ANDREW)). 1/2 in distribution antenna cable in plenum ceilings and anywhere a plenum cable is required. Air dielectric with PE spline. Installation temperature -5 C to +60 C (+23 F to +140 F) - cold-weather pulls limited. Single-bend radius not on the sheet.
- **AVA5-50** (HELIAX Virtual Air 7/8 in, CommScope HELIAX (now ANDREW)). Long 7/8 in donor/backbone runs outdoors or in raceway where loss matters more than size. 1-5000 MHz. Sheet revised 2018; confirm the current ANDREW revision when ordering.
- **AVA5RK-50** (HELIAX Virtual Air 7/8 in, fire-retardant jacket, CommScope HELIAX (now ANDREW)). 7/8 in riser backbone in buildings where CATVR is accepted (not plenum). Same attenuation as AVA5-50 on the sheet. 1-5000 MHz.
- **AVA6-50** (HELIAX Virtual Air 1-1/4 in, CommScope HELIAX (now ANDREW)). Very long trunk runs (campus, tunnel head-end, tower) where 7/8 in loss is too high. 1-3700 MHz.
- **AVA7-50** (HELIAX Virtual Air 1-5/8 in, CommScope HELIAX (now ANDREW)). Main feeders where the lowest loss is needed; heavy and stiff. Operating band 1-2700 MHz only - does not cover 3.x GHz.
- **LDF5-50A (obsolete)** (HELIAX LDF 7/8 in low-density foam, Andrew / CommScope). Legacy installs only - you will meet it in existing buildings; for new work order a current 7/8 in cable (e.g. AVA5-50 or RFS LCF78-50JA) after confirming availability. The CommScope sheet (2010) is marked OBSOLETE. OD differs by source: 1.030 in (CommScope 2010) vs 1.08 in (Andrew 2002); the 2002 sheet gives a 10 in minimum bend radius. Old Andrew connector part numbers on the 2002 sheet: L5PNM-RC (N male), L5PNF-RC (N female), L5PDM-RC (7-16 male), L5PDF-RC (7-16 female).
- **FSJ1-50A** (HELIAX superflexible 1/4 in, CommScope HELIAX (now ANDREW)). Short equipment jumpers and tight-bend pigtails only - high loss per foot. 1-18000 MHz. Sheet revised 2013.
- **LCF12-50J** (RFS CELLFLEX 1/2 in low-loss foam, RFS Technologies (an Amphenol company)). Outdoor 1/2 in jumpers and feed transitions; GPS lines. Max frequency 8.8 GHz; 38 kW peak. 150 MHz not tabulated (100 MHz 0.66, 200 MHz 0.95). Recommended/max clamp spacing 0.6/1 m (2/3.25 ft).
- **LCF78-50JA** (RFS CELLFLEX 7/8 in premium attenuation, RFS Technologies (an Amphenol company)). 7/8 in main feed lines outdoors / in raceway. Max frequency 5 GHz (title says supports CBRS/C-Band to 4.2 GHz); 85 kW peak; clamp spacing 0.8/1 m. 150 MHz not tabulated.
- **LCF78-50JFNA** (RFS CELLFLEX 7/8 in, flame-retardant halogen-free jacket, RFS Technologies (an Amphenol company)). Indoor 7/8 in riser/backbone where CATVR is accepted. Installation temperature -15 C to +60 C. Its table is slightly higher than the black-PE LCF78-50JA (0.99 vs 0.95 dB/100 ft at 700 MHz) - both sheets dated 2024-01-15.
- **LCF158-50JA** (RFS CELLFLEX 1-5/8 in premium attenuation, RFS Technologies (an Amphenol company)). Lowest-loss main feed lines. Max frequency 2.75 GHz; 310 kW peak.
- **SCF12-50J** (RFS CELLFLEX 1/2 in superflexible, RFS Technologies (an Amphenol company)). Superflexible jumpers between hardline and equipment/antennas. Max frequency 10.6 GHz; clamp spacing 0.3/0.5 m. Single-bend radius not on the sheet.
- **ICA12-50JPL** (RFS ClearFill Line plenum-rated 1/2 in air dielectric, RFS Technologies (an Amphenol company)). Indoor DAS 1/2 in distribution antenna cable in plenum spaces. Max frequency 6 GHz. Applications listed: wireless, mobile radio, in-building DAS.
- **HCA78-50JPL** (RFS 7/8 in plenum-rated air dielectric, RFS Technologies (an Amphenol company)). 7/8 in backbone through plenum spaces. Max frequency 4.2 GHz; 73 kW peak. No 2500/2600 MHz row on the sheet.
- **2HB12-50JPLR (DragonSkin)** (RFS 1/2 in 2-hour fire-resistive coax, RFS Technologies (an Amphenol company)). ERCES backbone in non-sprinklered, partially sprinklered or high-rise buildings where the backbone must resist fire without a rated shaft. Max frequency 1 GHz - cannot carry bands above 1 GHz. In-fire loss table used a 100 ft sample with 10 ft in the fire (1010 C / 1850 F). Clamp spacing 0.61 m (2 ft); the listing covers the whole system, so the BOM must include the listed supports.
- **LMR-400** (Times Microwave LMR flexible low-loss (0.405 in), Times Microwave Systems (Amphenol)). Short flexible jumpers and short antenna feeders; drop-in for RG-8 size. 700/850/1900/2600 MHz values are computed from the datasheet's own formula dB/100 ft = 0.122290*sqrt(F) + 0.000260*F (F in MHz), not tabulated; tabulated 150/450 shown as printed. The formula reproduces the tabulated 900 MHz value (3.9). Peak power 16 kW.
- **LMR-400-FR** (Times Microwave LMR, riser fire-rated, Times Microwave Systems (Amphenol)). In-building jumpers and short runs anywhere except plenums. Times says LMR-FR can be routed anywhere except air-handling plenums. Fairview's FR table matches LMR-400 (150 MHz 1.5, 450 MHz 2.7, 900 MHz 3.9). 700/850/1900/2600 computed from the LMR-400 formula. Fairview lists VoP 84%, the older Times sheet 85%.
- **LMR-400-LLPL** (Times Microwave LMR plenum, Times Microwave Systems (Amphenol)). Flexible jumpers and short runs inside return-air plenums (dropped ceilings, raised floors). Installation/operating temperature -5 C to +75 C only. 700/850/1900/2600 computed from the datasheet formula 0.129140*sqrt(F) + 0.000150*F. Plenum connectors on the sheet include TC-400-NF-PL (stock 3190-964).
- **LMR-400-LLPX** (Times Microwave LMR plenum, low PIM, Times Microwave Systems (Amphenol); sheet from L-com (Infinite Electronics)). Low-PIM plenum jumpers for in-building wireless / LMR. Values from the L-com sheet; no formula on that sheet, so 700/850/1900/2600 left null. OD 0.37 in per L-com.
- **LMR-600** (Times Microwave LMR flexible low-loss (0.590 in), Times Microwave Systems (Amphenol)). Longer flexible runs where 1/2 in hardline is awkward. 700/850/1900/2600 computed from the datasheet formula 0.075550*sqrt(F) + 0.000260*F; formula gives 2.5 at 900 MHz, matching the table. Fairview's LMR-600 sheet gives the same table (150 MHz 1.0, 450 MHz 1.7, 900 MHz 2.5, 1.8 GHz 3.7, 2.5 GHz 4.4). Stock codes read from a misaligned table; confirm when ordering.
- **RLKU12-50JFNA** (RFS RADIAFLEX radiating (leaky) cable 1/2 in, RLKU A-series, RFS Technologies (an Amphenol company)). Tunnels, parking structures, stairwells and long corridors where the cable itself is the antenna. STOP BANDS 650-750, 1330-1430, 2025-2100 MHz - no 700 MHz row; the 650-750 MHz stop band sits just below the 758-805 MHz public-safety 700 band and covers lower 700 MHz commercial frequencies; check with RFS before carrying 698-756 MHz. Mount at least 80 mm (3.15 in) from walls. Coupling loss tolerance +5 dB, measured free-space per IEC 61196-4; RFS warns in-building/tunnel performance may differ.
- **RLKU78-50JFNA** (RFS RADIAFLEX radiating cable 7/8 in, RLKU A-series, RFS Technologies (an Amphenol company)). Long tunnel / garage runs. Same stop bands as RLKU12 (650-750, 1330-1430, 2025-2100 MHz). Max 2700 MHz. 80 mm minimum distance to wall.
- **RLKU114-50JFLA** (RFS RADIAFLEX radiating cable 1-1/4 in, RLKU A-series, RFS Technologies (an Amphenol company)). Long tunnels where 7/8 in longitudinal loss is too high. Same stop bands as RLKU12. 80 mm minimum distance to wall.
- **RCT5-WBC-1X-RNA (RADIAX)** (CommScope/ANDREW RADIAX radiating cable 7/8 in, CommScope / ANDREW). Radiating cable alternative to RADIAFLEX, 50-2800 MHz per its title. NOT VERIFIED: only the datasheet title was readable (full sheet behind a sign-in). Get the current ANDREW datasheet before using it in a design.

### Hybrid coupler / combiner

## 5. ERCES / DAS code requirements that drive materials

Summaries are in plain words. The NFPA 1225 (2022) text was read from a copy of Chapter 18 posted by the Georgia Office of Commissioner of Insurance ([nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download)) and checked against NFPA's own second-revision report ([nfpa1225_sr](https://docinfofiles.nfpa.org/files/AboutTheCodes/1225/1225_Cust2021_PUF_AAA_SD_SRstatements.pdf)); the 2024 IFC text was read on ICC Digital Codes ([ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features)). Local amendments often add to these, so the AHJ's adopted edition and ERCES guideline decide.

### UL 2524 listing of active equipment

**Repeaters, transmitters, receivers, signal-booster components, remote annunciators and operational consoles, power supplies and battery charging components must be listed and labeled to UL 2524.** (NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.12.1.3; IFC 2024 510.4)

IFC 2024 510.4 says equipment providing in-building ERCES shall be listed in accordance with UL 2524. Passive parts (cable, splitters, couplers, antennas) are not on NFPA's UL 2524 list, but NFPA 1225 18.12.1.1 requires RF-emitting devices and cabling to be approved by the AHJ and the frequency license holder. UL describes UL 2524 as covering safety (fire, shock), performance against IFC-2018 / NFPA 1221 (2019), and marking/installation documents. Vendor sheets cite 2nd or 3rd Edition; a 3rd edition is cited by Fiplex but was not confirmed on a UL page.

Sources: [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features), [ul_erces](https://www.ul.com/services/emergency-responder-communications-enhancement-systems-erces)

### Enclosures for BDAs and batteries

**BDA, repeater, receiver, transmitter, fiber optical-to-RF/RF-to-optical converters and external filters in NEMA 4 or 4X enclosures; batteries in NEMA 3R or better; batteries that need venting in NEMA 3R.** (NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.3.1.1-18.3.1.3; IFC 2024 510.4.2.4 items 1-2)

IFC 2024 says 'NEMA Type 4 cabinet' for all signal booster components and NEMA 3R or higher for battery systems. The 2018 IFC wording was 'NEMA 4-type waterproof cabinet'. The BDAs and BBUs in the components list ship in their own NEMA 4 / Type 4 housings.

Sources: [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features), [ifc2018_510](https://callmc.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/IFC_510_Sheet-1.pdf)

### Secondary (standby) power

**12 hours at 100% system load: a dedicated battery, an AHJ-approved alternative, or a 2-hour battery plus a facility generator that can carry the full load for 12 hours.** (NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.13.2; IFC 2024 510.4.2.3)

Primary power must be a dedicated branch circuit, permanently connected, NFPA 72 compliant and protected from overvoltage (18.13.1). IFC 510.6.1 annual testing loads batteries for 1 hour. Size battery cabinets from the vendor's runtime table at the real load, not the nameplate Ah.

Sources: [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features)

### Supervisory signals to the fire alarm system

**The ERCES must be monitored by a listed fire alarm control unit (or, where approved, an audible signal at a constantly attended location).** (IFC 2024 510.4.2.5; NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.14.1)

IFC 2024 list: loss of normal AC, battery charger failure, signal source malfunction, active RF-emitting device failure, low battery at 70% of the 12-hour capacity depleted, failure of critical components, the FACU-to-ERCES communications link, and oscillation. A single supervisory input may be allowed where approved. NFPA 1225 requires NFPA 72 Chapter 10 integrity monitoring of the same list.

Sources: [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features), [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download)

### Dedicated annunciator

**A dedicated annunciator in the fire command center with visual, labeled indications per component and RF-emitting device.** (NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.14.2)

Indications: normal AC, loss of AC, battery charger failure, low battery (70% depletion), signal source malfunction, active RF-emitting device malfunction, active system component malfunction; the link to the annunciator is supervised. The annunciator is itself on the UL 2524 list (18.12.1.3).

Sources: [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download)

### Backbone survivability (fire resistance)

**Fully sprinklered (NFPA 13) buildings: backbone cables and components need no fire-resistance rating. Non-sprinklered, partially sprinklered or high-rise buildings: use a cable with a listed fire-resistance rating or run it in a protected enclosure/area.** (NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.12.3.3-18.12.3.6)

Required rating follows the building's primary structural frame: 2 hours where the frame needs 2 hours or more (or heavy timber); 1 hour where it needs less than 2; none where it needs none. If cables run in a rated enclosure, the backbone-to-distribution-antenna-cable connection must be inside it and penetrations firestopped to the same rating; not required when both backbone cable and components are themselves fire rated. The committee rejected metal conduit as fire protection. Example listed cable: RFS DragonSkin 2HB12-50JPLR (UL 2196 2 h, FHIT 1250, max 1 GHz).

Sources: [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [nfpa1225_sr](https://docinfofiles.nfpa.org/files/AboutTheCodes/1225/1225_Cust2021_PUF_AAA_SD_SRstatements.pdf), [rfs_2hb12_50jplr](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/pim/product/pdf/2HB12-50JPLR)

### Older-edition survivability rules (jurisdictions still on them)

**NFPA 72-2013 and NFPA 1221-2016: 2-hour riser coax. NFPA 1221-2019: backbone routed through an enclosure matching the building's fire rating.** (NFPA 72-2013 24.3.6.8; NFPA 1221-2016 9.6.2.1.x; NFPA 1221-2019 9.6.2.3 (as tabulated))

Taken from the Safer Buildings Coalition and Honeywell comparison tables, not the standards themselves. Ask the AHJ which edition is adopted.

Sources: [sbc_codes_2022](https://sbc.memberclicks.net/assets/docs/NFPA-Codes-2022.pdf), [honeywell_bda_wp](https://buildings.honeywell.com/content/dam/hbtbt/en/documents/downloads/BDA%20Whitepaper.pdf)

### Cable fire ratings (plenum / riser)

**All ERCES cables installed per NEC (NFPA 70) Chapters 7 and 8; the NEC sets which listed coax type goes where.** (NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.12.3.1; NEC Art. 820 (pre-2023 numbering))

NEC coax types: CATVP plenum (air-handling spaces; can substitute anywhere), CATVR riser (vertical runs through floors), CATV general purpose, CATVX limited use; vertical runs through more than one floor need CATVR or CATVP unless in metal raceway (EC&M, 2007-era NEC). Datasheets map tests to types: NFPA 262/UL 910 = CATVP/CMP; UL 1666 = CATVR/CMR. The SBC table summarizes NFPA 1225 as requiring plenum-rated backbone and distribution antenna cable; the 18.12.3.1 text itself only points to NEC Chapters 7 and 8 - many AHJs require plenum everywhere, so check local amendments. The 2023 NEC moved general Chapter 8 rules into Article 800; that numbering was not checked against NFPA 70 here.

Sources: [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [ecm_art820](https://www.ecmweb.com/national-electrical-code/code-basics/article/20885527/article-820-community-antenna-tv-and-radio-distribution-systems), [sbc_codes_2022](https://sbc.memberclicks.net/assets/docs/NFPA-Codes-2022.pdf), [cs_hl4rpv_50](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/HL4RPV-50.pdf), [cs_ldf4rk_50a](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/LDF4RK-50A.pdf), [rfs_lcf78_50jfna](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/pim/product/pdf/LCF78-50JFNA)

### Mechanical protection of coax

**Mechanical protection and raceways for coax per NEC Article 820.** (NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.12.3.2)

Committee statement: only cables subject to physical damage need protection; not all cable must be in conduit.

Sources: [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [nfpa1225_sr](https://docinfofiles.nfpa.org/files/AboutTheCodes/1225/1225_Cust2021_PUF_AAA_SD_SRstatements.pdf)

### Lightning / surge protection on the donor line

**Each donor antenna coax gets a listed antenna discharge unit per NEC Art. 820, listed to UL 497C; antenna, mast and discharge unit grounded per Art. 820.** (NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.4.1-18.4.4)

The 2022 edition dropped the earlier NFPA 780 reference because NFPA 780 covers the structure, not equipment. Check the arrestor's listing: several PolyPhaser sheets in the components list cite UL 497E, while NFPA 1225 names UL 497C - confirm with the vendor/AHJ before ordering.

Sources: [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [nfpa1225_sr](https://docinfofiles.nfpa.org/files/AboutTheCodes/1225/1225_Cust2021_PUF_AAA_SD_SRstatements.pdf), [pas_polyphaser_is50nxc2me](https://www.polyphaser.com/Images/Downloadables/Datasheets/IS-50NX-C2-ME_datasheets_US.pdf)

### Grounding the coax shield at entry

**Bond the coax outer conductor to the building grounding system as close as practicable to the point of entrance.** (NEC 820.100 (older numbering, as summarized by EC&M 2007))

Bonding conductor: insulated copper, at least 14 AWG, need not exceed 6 AWG, current capacity at least that of the outer conductor, as short and straight as practicable; terminate at the nearest accessible point of the grounding electrode system. Grounding kits (ANDREW SureGround SG12-12B2U / SG78-12B2U, RFS GKSPEED) use 6 AWG / 16 mm2 leads.

Sources: [ecm_art820](https://www.ecmweb.com/national-electrical-code/code-basics/article/20885527/article-820-community-antenna-tv-and-radio-distribution-systems), [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download)

### Donor antenna mounting and isolation

**Donor antennas permanently affixed, or on an approved movable sled with the 'movement or repositioning prohibited' sign; isolation between donor and all inside antennas at least 20 dB above system gain.** (NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.3.3, 18.10; IFC 2024 510.4.2.4 item 4, 510.5.1)

Antenna installation must keep the building envelope weather protection (IFC 510.5.1 / NFPA 18.3.3.3). High front-to-back donor antennas and physical separation help reach the isolation figure.

Sources: [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features)

### Oscillation control

**Signal boosters must have built-in oscillation detection and control that reduces gain and keeps operating; oscillation sends a supervisory signal; shutdown allowed only for uncorrectable oscillation.** (NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.3.2; IFC 2024 510.4.2.4 item 5)

IFC 510.5.4 also requires a spectrum analyzer check for spurious oscillation at acceptance and every annual test.

Sources: [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features)

### FCC Part 90 signal booster class, consent and registration

**Class A: no passband over 75 kHz (channelized). Class B: a passband over 75 kHz. Non-licensees need the licensee's express consent; Class B installations are registered in the FCC signal booster database; boosters are labeled as Class A/B and 'NOT a CONSUMER device'.** (47 CFR 90.219(a), (b)(1)(i), (d)(5), (e)(5); IFC 2024 510.5.2.1, 510.5.5)

IFC 2024 also requires written authorization from the frequency license holder before first activation of certified RF-emitting devices.

Sources: [cfr_90_219](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/90.219), [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features)

### Frequencies and upgradeability

**The system must carry every frequency the AHJ requires, any modulation in current use, and be upgradeable when frequencies change.** (NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.11; IFC 2024 510.4.2.6, 510.6.2)

Pick passives whose frequency range covers every band now and planned (e.g. VHF needs 138-174 MHz-capable couplers/antennas; many low-PIM DAS passives start at 350 MHz). NFPA notes VHF changes are hard because of filters and non-standard Tx/Rx spacing.

Sources: [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features)

### Near-far and antenna density

**Design to minimize near-far effects and include enough distribution antennas to address reduced-gain conditions.** (NFPA 1225 (2022) 18.3.4; IFC 2024 510.4.2.8)

More, lower-power antennas reduce the chance that one nearby portable drives the uplink into gain reduction. This drives antenna count and coupler values in the BOM.

Sources: [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features)

### Link budget: choosing cable size

**No code number; size cable from datasheet loss at the highest band carried, times run length.** (Design practice (datasheet arithmetic))

Example, 200 ft run at 700 MHz: LDF4-50A 1.831 dB/100 ft = 3.7 dB; AVA5-50 0.986 = 2.0 dB; LMR-400 3.4 (formula) = 6.8 dB. At 2000 MHz: LDF4-50A 3.251 = 6.5 dB vs AVA5-50 1.759 = 3.5 dB. Check every band: a run that is fine at 150 MHz can fail at 2.x GHz. Fire-resistive coax loses more in a fire (DragonSkin 700 MHz: 2.07 normal, 4.04 dB/100 ft with 10 ft burning).

Sources: [cs_ldf4_50a](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/LDF4-50A.pdf), [cs_ava5_50](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/AVA5-50.pdf), [times_lmr400](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/LMR-400.pdf), [rfs_2hb12_50jplr](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/pim/product/pdf/2HB12-50JPLR)

### Component power ratings

**Every passive must handle the composite average power at its position, at the highest band carried and the enclosure temperature.** (Design practice (vendor ratings))

Ratings vary widely: Norsat DDC240 coupler 50 W avg; RFS CDS couplers 200 W; Microlab CK-7x1 300 W; Microlab D2-61 Wilkinson 100 W; gas-tube arrestor PolyPhaser IS-50NX-C2 375 W at 125-220 MHz but 50 W at 700-1000 MHz; Microlab TK-27 load 100 W with derating above 55 C. Unused hybrid ports must be terminated at the right power rating (Microlab CA-PS).

Sources: [pas_norsat_ddc240_sheet](https://www.talleycom.com/images/PDF/SINDDC240.pdf), [pas_rfs_cds43_2700](https://www.rfsworld.com/product/CDS10-43-350/2700-01/pdf), [pas_microlab_ck7x1](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/ck-7x1.pdf), [pas_microlab_dx61](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/dx-61.pdf), [pas_polyphaser_is50nxc2me](https://www.polyphaser.com/Images/Downloadables/Datasheets/IS-50NX-C2-ME_datasheets_US.pdf), [pas_microlab_tk27](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/tk-27.pdf), [pas_microlab_caps196](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/ca-ps-2x2-196fn.pdf)

### Low-PIM choices for multi-carrier DAS

**Use components with a stated PIM rating, compared at the same test level (2x20 W = 2x43 dBm is the common one).** (Design practice (vendor ratings))

CommScope tests PIM with 2x20 W carriers in swept mode. Typical low-PIM ratings in this file: couplers/hybrids -160/-161 dBc (RFS, Microlab), loads -160/-161 dBc, RFS CELLFLEX -160 dBc; 4.3-10 166 dBc and NEX10 166 dBc connectors vs 160 dBc for 7-16/N (Rosenberger). Wilkinson splitters' resistors limit power and PIM (Microlab), so reactive splitters are preferred where low PIM matters. Sinclair rates its antenna at 2x3 W, so its -153 dBc is not comparable with 2x20 W ratings.

Sources: [commscope_pim_faq](https://wirelessestimator.com/publicdocs/PIM-FAQs.pdf), [rosenberger_43_10](https://www.rosenberger.com/fileadmin/content/headquarter/Downloads/RF_Coaxial_Connectors/COM_43_10_Catalog_2020.pdf), [pas_rfs_cds43_2700](https://www.rfsworld.com/product/CDS10-43-350/2700-01/pdf), [pas_microlab_ck7x1](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/ck-7x1.pdf), [pas_microlab_dx72](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/dx-72.pdf), [pas_sinclair_si240f](https://www.talleycom.com/images/PDF/SINSI240F-OWBL4F.pdf), [rfs_lcf78_50ja](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/pim/product/pdf/LCF78-50JA)

### Radiating cable stop bands

**Check radiating cable stop bands against every band carried.** (Design practice (datasheet))

## 6. Selection checklists

### Pick a feeder cable

*When:* For every coax run on the floor plan, before it goes on the BOM.

1. **Name the space the run passes through.** Air-handling plenum -> plenum (CATVP/CMP, NFPA 262) e.g. HL4RPV-50, ICA12-50JPL, HCA78-50JPL, LMR-400-LLPL. Vertical through floors -> riser (CATVR/CMR, UL 1666) or plenum, e.g. LDF4RK-50A, AVA5RK-50, LCF78-50JFNA, LMR-400-FR. Outdoor/roof -> PE-jacket hardline (LDF4-50A, AVA5-50, LCF78-50JA). ([ecm_art820](https://www.ecmweb.com/national-electrical-code/code-basics/article/20885527/article-820-community-antenna-tv-and-radio-distribution-systems), [cs_hl4rpv_50](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/HL4RPV-50.pdf), [cs_ldf4rk_50a](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/LDF4RK-50A.pdf), [rfs_lcf78_50jfna](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/pim/product/pdf/LCF78-50JFNA), [times_lmr400](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/LMR-400.pdf))
2. **Is this run backbone in a non-sprinklered, partially sprinklered or high-rise building?.** If yes, it needs a listed fire-resistive cable (e.g. DragonSkin 2HB12-50JPLR, UL 2196 2 h) or a rated enclosure/shaft at 2 h / 1 h per the structural frame rating. DragonSkin tops out at 1 GHz. ([nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [rfs_2hb12_50jplr](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/pim/product/pdf/2HB12-50JPLR))
3. **Check the frequency ceiling.** Datasheet operating band must cover every band carried: AVA7-50 stops at 2700 MHz, LCF158-50JA 2.75 GHz, DragonSkin 1 GHz, RADIAFLEX RLKU 2700 MHz with stop bands. ([cs_ava7_50](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/AVA7-50.pdf), [rfs_lcf158_50ja](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/pim/product/pdf/LCF158-50JA), [rfs_2hb12_50jplr](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/pim/product/pdf/2HB12-50JPLR), [rfs_rlku12_50jfna](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/pim/product/pdf/RLKU12-50JFNA))
4. **Compute loss at the highest band.** Length/100 x dB/100 ft from the datasheet, plus connector and passive losses; move up a size (1/2 -> 7/8 in) if the downlink or uplink budget fails. ([cs_ldf4_50a](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/LDF4-50A.pdf), [cs_ava5_50](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/AVA5-50.pdf))
5. **Check bend radius and route.** Compare minimum bend radius with sleeves, trays and turns (e.g. 7/8 in AVA5-50: 5 in single / 10 in multiple; LMR-400: 1 in installation / 4 in repeated). ([cs_ava5_50](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/AVA5-50.pdf), [times_lmr400](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/LMR-400.pdf))
6. **Check installation temperature.** Plenum air-dielectric and plenum LMR have narrow ranges (HL4RPV-50 install from -5 C; LMR-400-LLPL -5 to +75 C). ([cs_hl4rpv_50](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/HL4RPV-50.pdf), [times_lmr400llpl](https://tessco-product-images-commerce-cloud-prod.s3.amazonaws.com/Resources/68254_LMR400-491178.pdf))
7. **PIM rating for multi-carrier systems.** Prefer cable with a stated PIM figure (RFS CELLFLEX -160 dBc) or a low-PIM flexible (LMR-400-LLPX) where cellular and public safety share the DAS. ([rfs_lcf78_50ja](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/pim/product/pdf/LCF78-50JA), [times_lmr400llpx_lcom](https://www.l-com.com/images/Downloadables/Datasheets/ds_LMR-400-LLPX-LC.pdf))
8. **Add support hardware.** Clamp spacing from the sheet (LCF12-50J 0.6/1 m; DragonSkin 2 ft) - for fire-rated cable the listed support system is part of the listing. ([rfs_lcf12_50j](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/pim/product/pdf/LCF12-50J), [rfs_2hb12_50jplr](https://www.rfstechnologies.com/pim/product/pdf/2HB12-50JPLR))

### Pick connectors

*When:* After the cable list is fixed, for every cable end and equipment port.

1. **Match the connector to the exact cable.** Use the cable maker's connector for that cable (e.g. Times EZ-400-NMH-X for LMR-400; plenum TC-400-NF-PL for LMR-400-LLPL). ([times_lmr400](https://www.talleycom.com/images/pdf/LMR-400.pdf), [times_lmr400llpl](https://tessco-product-images-commerce-cloud-prod.s3.amazonaws.com/Resources/68254_LMR400-491178.pdf))
2. **Match the equipment port interface.** Read each BDA, coupler, splitter and antenna port (N, 4.3-10, 7-16, NEX10) and minimize adapters. ([te_rf_qrg](https://www.farnell.com/datasheets/3170513.pdf))
3. **Choose by PIM need.** Multi-carrier/low-PIM paths: 4.3-10 (166 dBc) or NEX10 (166 dBc); 7-16 (160 dBc) for high-power legacy; N is common on public-safety gear but its male outer contact is easily damaged. ([rosenberger_43_10](https://www.rosenberger.com/fileadmin/content/headquarter/Downloads/RF_Coaxial_Connectors/COM_43_10_Catalog_2020.pdf), [rosenberger_nex10](https://www.rosenberger.com/product/nex10/), [anritsu_understanding_pim](https://dl.cdn-anritsu.com/en-us/test-measurement/files/Application-Notes/Application-Note/11410-00629F.pdf))
4. **Check frequency ceiling.** 4.3-10 is specified to 6 GHz, 7-16 to 7 GHz (TE), N to 11 GHz, NEX10 to 20 GHz. ([iec_61169_54](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/25812), [te_rf_qrg](https://www.farnell.com/datasheets/3170513.pdf), [rosenberger_nex10](https://www.rosenberger.com/product/nex10/))
5. **Keep 75-ohm parts out.** F and 75-ohm BNC parts do not belong in a 50-ohm DAS path. ([iec_61169_24](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/19282), [te_rf_qrg](https://www.farnell.com/datasheets/3170513.pdf))
6. **List the torque tools.** 4.3-10 screw 5 N-m (22 mm), NEX10 ~1.5 N-m (11 mm), 7-16 25-30 N-m, N per maker (0.7-1.1 N-m Rosenberger). ([rosenberger_43_10](https://www.rosenberger.com/fileadmin/content/headquarter/Downloads/RF_Coaxial_Connectors/COM_43_10_Catalog_2020.pdf), [andrew_tw_n10](https://www.andrew.com/products/fiber-power-rf-connectivity-accessories/accessories/installation-tools/itemtw-n10/), [microwaves101_torque](https://www.microwaves101.com/encyclopedias/connector-torque))
7. **Weatherproofing for outdoor joints.** Add weatherproofing kits/tape for roof connections; Anritsu lists cleaning, assembly, weather wrapping and correct torque as the fixes for connector PIM. ([anritsu_understanding_pim](https://dl.cdn-anritsu.com/en-us/test-measurement/files/Application-Notes/Application-Note/11410-00629F.pdf))

### Pick a splitter / coupler / hybrid

*When:* For each node in the distribution tree.

1. **Frequency range covers every band.** Many low-PIM DAS passives start at 350 MHz (RFS CDS/TPS, Microlab CK-7x1) - for VHF use parts like Norsat DDC240 (138-960 MHz), Microlab D2-61 (70-2700 MHz) or Microlab CA-PS-2x2-196 (128-1000 MHz). ([pas_rfs_cds43_2700](https://www.rfsworld.com/product/CDS10-43-350/2700-01/pdf), [pas_microlab_ck7x1](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/ck-7x1.pdf), [pas_norsat_ddc240_sheet](https://www.talleycom.com/images/PDF/SINDDC240.pdf), [pas_microlab_dx61](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/dx-61.pdf), [pas_microlab_caps196](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/ca-ps-2x2-196fn.pdf))
2. **Power rating vs composite power.** Average rating must exceed the composite power at that point (e.g. DDC240 50 W vs CK-7x1 300 W). ([pas_norsat_ddc240_sheet](https://www.talleycom.com/images/PDF/SINDDC240.pdf), [pas_microlab_ck7x1](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/ck-7x1.pdf))
3. **Coupling value and through loss.** Take coupling from the design; note through (insertion) loss (CDS family: 1.8/0.8/0.5/0.25/0.3 dB for 6/10/15/20/30 dB). ([pas_rfs_cds43_2700](https://www.rfsworld.com/product/CDS10-43-350/2700-01/pdf))
4. **Splitter type.** Reactive splitters carry more power at lower PIM; Wilkinson splitters isolate outputs (>=20 dB) but resistors limit power and PIM. ([pas_microlab_dx08](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/dx-08.pdf), [pas_microlab_dx72](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/dx-72.pdf))
5. **PIM at a stated test level.** Compare -153 / -160 / -161 dBc only at the same tone power (2x43 dBm). ([pas_rfs_cds43_2700](https://www.rfsworld.com/product/CDS10-43-350/2700-01/pdf), [commscope_pim_faq](https://wirelessestimator.com/publicdocs/PIM-FAQs.pdf))
6. **Terminate unused ports.** Hybrid unused outputs need a load at the right power rating (Microlab CA-PS); low-PIM loads e.g. Microlab TK-27, Radiall R404DE5050. ([pas_microlab_caps196](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/ca-ps-2x2-196fn.pdf), [pas_microlab_tk27](https://microlabtech.com/pub/media/asset/Datasheets/tk-27.pdf), [pas_radiall_r404de5050](https://radiall-files.s3.amazonaws.com/tds/microwave/R404DE5050_14-09-21A.pdf))
7. **Connector and environment.** Same interface family as the cable plan; IP65/IP67 where needed. ([pas_rfs_cds43_2700](https://www.rfsworld.com/product/CDS10-43-350/2700-01/pdf))
8. **Approval.** Passives are not on the UL 2524 list but need AHJ and license-holder approval. ([nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download))

### ERCES material compliance check

*When:* Before submitting the design/BOM to the AHJ and again before ordering.

1. **UL 2524 on every active part.** BDA/repeater, fiber master/remotes, BBU/charger, power supplies and annunciator - and confirm the listing names the exact model combination (e.g. ADRF BBU listed with named PSR models). ([nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features), [act_wcw_adrf_bbsu24](https://harrier.smartwire.com/SpecSheet/ADRF-BBS-U-24.pdf))
2. **Enclosure ratings.** NEMA 4/4X for BDA, repeaters, optical converters, external filters; NEMA 3R+ for batteries. ([nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features))
3. **12-hour secondary power at 100% load.** Use the vendor runtime table at the real load (Comba: hours = 48.1 x Ah / load W; one ADRF 48 Ah BBU runs 5.8-11.4 h on its repeaters). ([nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [act_comba_v3ng](https://www.alliancecorporation.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/RXxxV3-RHxxV3-NG-700-800MHz-Public-Safety-BDA-DAS-27-33dBm-and-Battery-Backup-Unit-DS-0-2-6.pdf), [act_wcw_adrf_bbsu24](https://harrier.smartwire.com/SpecSheet/ADRF-BBS-U-24.pdf))
4. **Supervisory points and annunciator.** Wire every IFC/NFPA supervisory signal to the FACU; dedicated annunciator in the fire command center. ([ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features), [nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download))
5. **Backbone fire rating.** Sprinklered: none required. Non-/partially sprinklered or high-rise: listed fire-resistive cable or rated enclosure (2 h / 1 h per structural frame); connection inside the enclosure; firestop penetrations. ([nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download))
6. **Donor line protection.** Listed antenna discharge unit (UL 497C) on each donor coax; ground antenna, mast and ADU per NEC 820; check the arrestor's UL category. ([nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [pas_polyphaser_is50nxc2me](https://www.polyphaser.com/Images/Downloadables/Datasheets/IS-50NX-C2-ME_datasheets_US.pdf))
7. **Donor isolation and mounting.** >= 20 dB above system gain; permanent mount or signed sled. ([nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features))
8. **Cable listings for each space.** Per NEC Ch. 7-8 and any local plenum-everywhere amendment. ([nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download), [ecm_art820](https://www.ecmweb.com/national-electrical-code/code-basics/article/20885527/article-820-community-antenna-tv-and-radio-distribution-systems), [sbc_codes_2022](https://sbc.memberclicks.net/assets/docs/NFPA-Codes-2022.pdf))
9. **FCC items.** Class A vs B chosen with the license holder; licensee consent on file; Class B registered; written authorization before activation; boosters labeled. ([cfr_90_219](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/90.219), [ifc2024_510](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IFC2024V2.0/chapter-5-fire-service-features))
10. **Frequency list.** Get the AHJ/license-holder frequency list and confirm every component covers it. ([nfpa1225_ch18](https://oci.georgia.gov/document/document/nfpa-1225-chapter-18/download))

## 7. Gaps and things not verified

- CommScope/ANDREW and RFS loss tables skip 850, 1900 and 2600 MHz on most cables; those cells are null in the JSON and the nearest listed rows are given instead. No interpolation was done.
- CommScope HELIAX sheets used here are 2013-2018 revisions hosted by Talley; current ANDREW sheets may differ slightly. ANDREW product pages were not reachable for every part.
- CommScope/ANDREW RADIAX (RCT5) radiating cable: only the datasheet title could be read; no losses or coupling loss recorded.
- No CommScope/ANDREW plenum 7/8 in HELIAX sheet was found; the 7/8 in plenum example is RFS HCA78-50JPL.
- NEX10 torque: ANDREW's TW-N10 wrench is 1.505 N-m; a 3 N-m figure appears in secondary sources but was not confirmed on a maker page.
- F-connector torque and a QMA IEC sectional-spec number were not found in a maker or IEC document.
- NEC (NFPA 70) text was not opened; plenum/riser rules come from a 2007 EC&M article (Article 820 numbering) and datasheet listings. 2023 NEC Article 800 renumbering not checked.
- NFPA 1225 was read from a state-posted copy of Chapter 18 (it notes TIAs/errata may apply), not from NFPA's own published PDF. NFPA 72 and UL 2524 full texts were not opened; UL 2524 3rd edition (2024) is cited by vendor sheets but was not confirmed on a UL page.
- The SBC code table says NFPA 1225 18.12.3.1 requires plenum-rated backbone and distribution cable; the 18.12.3.1 text read here only says to install cables per NEC Chapters 7 and 8. Treat plenum-everywhere as an AHJ question.
- PolyPhaser IS-50NX-C2 and TSX-4310FM-P sheets cite UL 497E; NFPA 1225 18.4.2 names UL 497C for antenna discharge units. Confirm the listing before ordering.
- Component gaps (from the parts research): no low-PIM coupler covering VHF through 2700 MHz; no low-PIM attenuator below 617 MHz; no Galtronics, Amphenol, JMA or Comba antenna sheet opened; no mount ballast tables; BDA sheets not opened for JMA, Westell/CSI, SureCall/Potter, Bird, ANDREW ERA/ION, Zinwave; no NEMA 4X BDA found (all NEMA 4 / Type 4); no UL 2196 fiber cable found.
- Supplier gaps: no RF/DAS distributor named "Fullerton" was identified (closest is Fullerton Consultants, a DAS engineering firm); Winncom public-safety catalog not reachable; Honeywell-Fiplex closing date not found.
